Team Policies
 
Your team will have a number of responsibilities as it completes problem and project assignments.

Designate a coordinator, recorder, and checker for each assignment. Add a monitor for 4-person teams. Rotate these roles for every assignment.
Each team must have 4 students. Each student (team member) must have a technical role. These technical roles are associated with project development responsibilities. Your team must have a backend developer, a frontend designer and developer, and a database engineer. A team member may have more than one role based on the iteration or can change the role between iterations. For example, database work is less than 25% of the project total work, the database engineer may help with frontend or backend development.
Each team must designate a team leader. The team leader must have one of the above technical roles.
All team members must participate in the project documentation.
 Break the work according to technical roles (skills) and the needed skills of the assigned work. You need to look ahead to the next deliverable besides the current one to make a fair distribution of the work. For example, deliverable 3  is mostly done by the web developer(s)/ frontend engineer, we might give the web developer less work in deliverable 2 so that she/he can start deliverable 3 earlier and complete it on time.
You need to share your work and make sure that you can integrate the code. Some Programming hints, tools, and techniques are in the project folder on eLC. You need to agree on these as well. (for example, the team will use Git as a version control system).
Agree on a common meeting time and what each member should have done before the meeting (readings, taking the first cut at some or all of the assigned work, etc.)
Do the required individual preparation.
The team leader checks with other team members before the meeting to remind them of when and where they will meet and what they are supposed to do.
Meet and work.
The Coordinator keeps everyone on task and makes sure everyone is involved.
The Recorder prepares the final solution to be turned in.
The Monitor checks to make sure everyone understands both the solution and the strategy used to get it.
The Checker double-checks it before it is handed in. Agree on the next meeting time and roles for the next assignment.
You may NOT follow the above roles, that is fine. However, you must have a common policy and procedure for the work to do it successfully!

Project deliverables are documents and coding assignments. Both should be submitted to eLC.
For coding assignments, a presentation/demo to the instructor or the TA is required. All demos will be over Zoom. We will share with you a presentation schedule and your team must signup for a demo time. You need to designate at least two members to demonstrate the working product. Change presenters based on the deliverable. (for example, web developers may present the UI design, the database designer and a java/python developer will present the database design).

The actual due date of deliverables that will be graded by a demonstration is your demo date/time.


The Team leader must submit the assignment to eLC and checks that the names on the cover page are of every team member who participated actively in completing it.

If you designated a checker, then it is the responsibility of the checker to check that the names on the cover page are of every team member who participated actively in completing it.

 If the team leader or the checker anticipates a problem in submitting the online submission to eLC before the due date, it is his/her responsibility to make sure someone submits it.

All online submitted files should follow the naming format: TeamN_Deliverable Y, where N is the team number and Y is the deliverable number.

Review graded assignments and feedback. Make sure everyone understands why points were lost and how to correct errors.

Consult with your instructor if a conflict arises that can’t be worked through by the team.

Dealing with non-cooperative team members:


If a team member refuses to cooperate on an assignment, his/ her name should not be included in the completed work. If the problem persists, the team should meet with the instructor so that the problem can be resolved, if possible. If the problem still continues, the cooperating team members may notify the uncooperative member in writing that he/she is in danger of being fired, sending a copy of the memo to the instructor. If there is no subsequent improvement, they should notify the individual in writing (copy to the instructor) that he/she is no longer with the team. The fired student should meet with his/her instructor to discuss options. Similarly, students who are consistently doing all the work for their team may issue a warning memo that they will quit unless they start getting cooperation and a second memo quitting the team if the cooperation is not forthcoming. Students who get fired or quit must either find another team willing to add them as a member or get zeroes for the remaining project assignments.

YOUR TEAM CONTRACT SHOULD ADHERE TO THE ABOVE POLICIES.
